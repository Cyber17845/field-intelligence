RULE
FMCSA pulled 133 electronic logging devices from its registry on November 12, and drivers on a revoked device from November 24 can be cited and put out of service.
FMCSA announced November 12 that it removed 133 devices from its Registered Devices List: 121 revoked by the agency and 12 voluntarily self-revoked after failing its readiness standards. The release calls it the largest ELD revocation in FMCSA history. The revoked devices are listed at eld.fmcsa.dot.gov.
Carriers have up to 60 days to replace a revoked device with a compliant one. After 60 days, a carrier still using a revoked device is treated as operating without an ELD. For drivers using a revoked device on or after November 24, safety officials are told to cite 395.8(a)(1) and place the driver out of service under CVSA criteria.
If a provider corrects every identified deficiency, FMCSA will restore the device to the registered list and notify the industry. The release describes three anonymized examples, ELD Company A, B and C.
The exposure is any fleet whose drivers log hours on an ELD, so check each truck's device against the list, serial number by serial number.
​
What to do about it: Today, record the make, model and serial number of the ELD in every truck and check each against the revoked list at eld.fmcsa.dot.gov. If a unit is listed, email your provider asking in writing whether and when it will be restored, and get a replacement quote and install date on paper. Do not plan around the 60 days: roadside officers are told to act on revoked devices from November 24. Tell dispatch which trucks are affected and book replacement installs for the first week of December at the latest. File the provider's reply with your driver records.

PEST CONTROL
Two items
​
Demand
NPMA's pest index names Washington and Norfolk, Va., among cities where ticks and rodents may stay active into winter, and Orkin ranks D.C. fifth and Baltimore tenth for rats.
​
Why it matters: NPMA's Public Health Pest Index, reported November 23, lists Washington, D.C., and Norfolk, Va., among eight cities where warm weather and heavy rain could keep pests such as mosquitoes, ticks and rodents active into winter. Orkin's 2026 list, reported November 18, puts D.C. fifth, up one spot, and Baltimore tenth, up two; Los Angeles is first. Call your rodent and tick customers this week and offer winter service before the holidays.
PMP: NPMA names cities at highest pest risk (Nov 23) · PMP: Orkin's rattiest cities list (Nov 18)
​
Demand
A LeadTruffle analysis of 4,699 contractor lead conversations found first replies that cite the request and ask one question drew a 60.5% response within 24 hours, against 50.3% for other replies.
​
Why it matters: The study, reported November 10, covered 87 home service accounts on Yelp, Thumbtack and Google Local Services Ads. The lift was 1.3 points on Yelp, 4.2 on Thumbtack and 5.4 on Google. On Thumbtack, generic wording such as "your request" was linked to a 14.3-point drop in replies. It is observational, measures replies and not bookings, and comes from a seller of AI lead-response software. Rewrite your first-reply template to name the job and ask one question.
PMP: Study finds optimum responses to sales leads

SEPTIC & SEWER
Two items
​
Demand
Roto-Rooter says plumbing calls jump 50% the day after Thanksgiving, with 21% more business over the holiday weekend, and Service Experts logged 15% more calls that Friday in 2025, Scripps reported November 14.
​
Why it matters: Scripps cites Roto-Rooter, saying volume rises 21% over Thanksgiving weekend against other four-day weekends, and notes Mr. Rooter has called the day after Thanksgiving its busiest of the year. Plumbers name grease and food waste in drains, higher household water use and existing drainage problems, including main sewer line blockages. The figures are company-reported. Over Thanksgiving week, staff Friday and the weekend for drain and main-line calls, and tell septic customers to keep grease out of drains and space showers when guests stay.
Scripps News: plumbers brace for busy Brown Friday
​
Regulation
Snohomish County, Wash., has required septic inspections at real estate sales since November 1, a statewide transfer program starts February 1, 2027, and Mashpee, Mass., adds septic rules December 1.
​
Why it matters: Onsite Installer's November 16 column says Snohomish inspections must be done by a certified septic professional within one year of closing. Mashpee's Board of Health approved rules that add Zone II areas, require innovative or alternative systems for new construction, and require upgrades for waterfront and Zone II properties from December 1. The column also reports a $2.3 million USDA award to NOWRA to help rural communities find and fix septic failures. Check your certification before taking Washington transfer inspections, and ask Mashpee's health office which systems it will approve.
Onsite Installer: NOWRA shares in $40M USDA award

APPLIANCE REPAIR
Two items
​
Money
BLS's October CPI, released November 10, shows appliance prices up 1.3% seasonally adjusted from September, though unadjusted prices were unchanged, and 12-month changes are unavailable for October.
​
Why it matters: BLS says it collected no October 2025 survey data, so it cannot publish October 2026 12-month changes for indexes that lack that base. The appliances index was unchanged unadjusted (0.0%) and rose 1.3% seasonally adjusted; the household furnishings and operations index rose 0.2% seasonally adjusted. One month of seasonally adjusted change is not a trend. Compare your October parts invoices with September and update your flat-rate sheet where a supplier moved prices.

Building material and garden dealers rose 0.5% on the month and 5.6% on the year. Advance figures come from a sample of about 4,800 firms and are revised; the next release is December 16. Compare your October repair bookings with September and last October before you set January technician hours and parts orders.

MOVING
Two items
​
Regulation
FMCSA announced November 6 an enhanced Complaint Center, the renamed National Consumer Complaint Database, with online filing and a toll-free line open weekdays.
​
Why it matters: FMCSA says the Complaint Center takes online submissions at complaint.fmcsa.dot.gov and calls at 1-888-368-7238, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern, Monday through Friday. ATA said April 30 that thousands of complaints against disreputable movers are filed each year. Review every open customer dispute this week, answer each in writing, and confirm that customers receive the two consumer documents ATA names, "Ready to Move?" and "Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move".
FMCSA: Complaint Center enhanced (Nov 6) · ATA: National Moving Month consumer tips (Apr 30)
​
Regulation
FMCSA suspended enforcement of biennial USDOT updates and stopped deactivating numbers for missed filings, for deadlines on or after June 1, 2026, while its Motus system settles in; relief runs until further notice.
​
Why it matters: FreightWaves reported September 9 that Motus launched May 19 and that FMCSA will give advance warning before enforcement resumes. The filing requirement remains. FMCSA's registration page says safety registration, docket-number elimination and BOC-3 changes remain under consideration for a proposed rule with public comment. Look up your biennial update due date in Motus, file it anyway, and keep the confirmation with your authority records.

CYBER RISK, PLAINLY
Ransomware, state hackers and AI scams: what small firms face
Criminals go where the money and customer data are. Ransomware is software that locks your files until you pay. The FBI got 3,600-plus ransomware complaints in 2025. Of 1,400-plus from firms outside critical industries, 18% were legal services firms and 17% contractors like electricians.
State-linked: US agencies assess that Iran-affiliated hackers altered equipment controllers at water, energy and local-government sites (updated July 2026). An August advisory lists commercial facilities among sectors being probed. A September fact sheet describes foreign hackers who entered an automation firm in 2025. AI scams: 22,364 FBI complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with losses over $893 million. Cloned voices can ask for wire payments.
In your trade: Scammers can clone a voice; if a caller posing as a customer or your office asks for a rush payment, hang up and call a number you already have.
​
What to do about it: Turn on two-step sign-in wherever you can, starting with email and admins. Keep a backup on a drive not connected to your network, and test it. Confirm any change in bank details by phone, on a number you already had. If a call asks for money, hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself. Install updates. Give people only the access they need. Know who to call; report fraud at ic3.gov. The part this doesn't cover, how your systems are set up, is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report; US government advisories (updated Jul 22, Aug and Sep 23, 2026); FBI IC3 alerts; CISA and FTC guidance.
