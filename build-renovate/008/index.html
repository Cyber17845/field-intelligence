RULE
Labor's final rule, announced November 19 and published in the Federal Register November 23, rebuilds the independent-contractor test around two factors, control over the work and opportunity for profit or loss, and takes effect January 22, 2027.
The Department of Labor announced the final rule November 19. It rescinds and replaces the 2024 rule that weighed six factors to decide whether a worker is an employee or an independent contractor under the Fair Labor Standards Act. The new test rests on two core factors: how much control the worker has over the work, and the worker's opportunity for profit or loss through initiative, investment or both.
Three other factors, skill required, permanence of the relationship and whether the work is part of an integrated unit of production, count only to the extent they show whether a worker is in business for themselves. Worker classification stays a fact-specific inquiry, and an independent-contractor agreement alone does not decide a worker's status.
The test also governs the Family and Medical Leave Act and the Migrant and Seasonal Agricultural Worker Protection Act. It does not change IRS or National Labor Relations Act assessments, and DOL included a number of fact-specific examples, including delivery workers and truck drivers. State tests can differ materially; Littler cites California's ABC test.
The rule was published in the Federal Register November 23, 2026 and takes effect January 22, 2027. ABC, whose suit over the 2024 rule is held in abeyance while DOL does new rulemaking, called the new rule a workable, welcome change.
​
What to do about it: List every crew and individual you pay on a 1099 and, for each, write down who sets the schedule and methods, who supplies tools, truck and insurance, and whether the worker bids jobs at their own risk of profit or loss. Pull the signed subcontract, W-9 and certificate of insurance for each one and fix the gaps now. Do not move employees to 1099 on the strength of this rule: it leaves IRS and NLRB tests unchanged, and state tests can differ. Ask your employment lawyer which test applies in each state you work in and get the answer in writing. Put January 22, 2027, the effective date, on your calendar.
ABC statement on DOL final rule (Nov 19, 2026) · Littler: DOL releases final independent contractor rule · Construction Dive: DOL flips independent contractor test

ROOFING
Two items
​
Supply
QXO, the second-largest roofing products distributor, reported November 12 that companywide third-quarter pricing rose 2.2% from a year earlier and complementary product sales in Roofing and Waterproofing grew 4.2%.
​
Why it matters: QXO's release puts third-quarter net sales at $4,895 million against $2,728 million a year earlier, with $1.64 billion from TopBuild and $589 million from Kodiak, so much of the jump is acquired. Companywide pricing rose 2.2% year over year, and QXO said organic daily revenue trends improved for a second quarter "despite a soft construction market." If QXO supplies you, ask your branch in writing for first-quarter 2027 shingle and accessory pricing and rebate terms before you quote spring jobs.
QXO third-quarter 2026 results (Business Wire, Nov 12)
​
Regulation
Brooklyn prosecutors indicted the owner of a closed New York training firm, RedCode, for selling Site Safety Training and OSHA cards at $500 each with no training; about 2,000 workers' cards were voided.
​
Why it matters: NRCA reported November 17 that city investigators posed as construction workers and were offered the cards for $500 each. The Brooklyn District Attorney charged RedCode's owner and an associate with offering a false instrument for filing, receiving reward for official misconduct and falsifying business records. About 2,000 workers hold RedCode-issued Site Safety Training cards, and all are invalidated. If you hire or sub work in New York City, check the issuing provider on every card and pull any from RedCode.
NRCA: Training providers charged with selling fake cards

RESTORATION
Two items
​
Demand
NFPA counted an estimated 1,139 home cooking fires on Thanksgiving Day 2024, 321% above the daily average, and Christmas Day and Christmas Eve ranked second and third at roughly twice the daily average.
​
Why it matters: Carrier Management reported November 16, citing NFPA, that 80% of Thanksgiving fires from 2020 to 2024 involved cooking equipment and that cooking causes about 48% of reported U.S. home fires. Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26, and NFPA's next-highest days are December 24 and 25. Before Wednesday, put a named on-call technician, project manager and owner of the after-hours phone on the schedule for November 26 and for December 24 and 25, and check that pack-out boxes, deodorization products and cleaning supplies are stocked for fire and smoke calls.
Carrier Management: Thanksgiving cooking fires (Nov 16)
​
Money
Helixco launched HydroTrac on November 11, a drying-documentation tool bundled free with its time-and-material billing software that ties moisture readings, photos and equipment logs to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.
​
Why it matters: HydroTrac attaches meter photos with capture times and technician details, charts readings against drying goals and builds one job report. Helixco frames it around carrier, third-party administrator and adjuster review of invoices, and its CEO says, "A lack of documentation results in a lack of payment." It is included at no extra cost with Helixco's T&M billing software and is available now. Pull your last three water-loss files that a carrier cut or delayed and list which readings, photos or equipment days were missing; weigh any documentation tool against that list.
R&R: Helixco launches HydroTrac (Nov 11)

GARAGE DOOR
Two items
​
Demand
Griffon, the Clopay garage door maker, guided fiscal 2027 revenue flat at about $1.85 billion on November 17, with residential volume expected to fall and price and mix offsetting it.
​
Why it matters: Griffon, which makes Clopay, IDEAL and Holmes doors, put fourth-quarter revenue up 3% to $490.9 million, with price and mix adding 6% and volume down 3% in both residential and commercial. Its CFO said the overall market has shrunk, mostly in lower-spec doors tied to weaker new construction. Griffon raised prices in its fiscal first quarter, October through December; the increase phases in over the first half of fiscal 2027 and is fully in place in the second half. Ask your Clopay or IDEAL rep for the effective date and percent, and re-quote open bids that run past it.
Griffon fiscal 2026 results release (Nov 2026) · Griffon Q4 2026 earnings call transcript (Nov 17)
​
Supply
Nucor's hot-rolled coil bid rose $15 to $1,310 a short ton on November 16, and spot coil was quoted at $1,350 to $1,400 on November 13 against $830 to $865 a year earlier.
​
Why it matters: GMK Center reported November 17 that Nucor's bid is $1,310 a short ton, up $15 from the prior week, with lead times unchanged at three to five weeks. Kallanish put U.S. spot coil at $1,350 to $1,400 on November 13 and at $830 to $865 in the same week of 2025. An SMU survey found buyers reporting longer lead times and rising prices amid stable or growing demand, with imports still competitive. Ask your door supplier how long its steel-door quotes hold, and write an expiry date on every steel-door proposal you send before year-end.
GMK Center: Nucor raises hot-rolled coil bid (Nov 17) · GMK Center: Nucor hot-rolled coil bid (Nov 10) · GMK Center: Nucor hot-rolled coil bid (Oct 6)

EXTERIORS
Two items
​
Money
Marvin's price increases of about 5% on Ultimate and Elevate windows and doors and about 3% on its Essential line took effect November 16, and AZEK Exteriors rose about 4.5% with shipments on November 2.
​
Why it matters: A distributor's November update, published November 2, lists Marvin Ultimate and Elevate Collection windows and doors up about 5% and the Essential Collection up about 3%, both effective November 16, and AZEK Exteriors up about 4.5% with shipments on November 2. It also lists Rockwool insulation up about 6% on January 1, 2027. The same update also lists Marvin's roughly 10% cut on its Essential window and patio door line, with no effective date. Compare open window and door quotes with the new list; for jobs not yet ordered, ask your distributor which price applies and get it in writing.
Universal Supply: November Monthly Update (Nov 2)
​
Supply
October producer prices put softwood plywood up 24.5% and particleboard and fiberboard up 13.9% from a year earlier, while building materials overall rose 0.5% in the month and 5.8% on the year.
​
Why it matters: NAHB's analysis of the BLS producer price index, posted November 13, shows building materials up 0.5% in October and 5.8% over twelve months. Softwood veneer and plywood rose 3.6% in the month and particleboard and fiberboard 5.4%. ABC's analysis of the same release, relayed by NRCA November 17, puts softwood lumber down 3.9% in October but up 10.6% on the year, and iron and steel up 2.5% in the month and 25.1% on the year. Reprice any spring bid that assumes last summer's plywood or steel cost, and add an escalation clause to contracts that run past 30 days.
NAHB Eye on Housing: building material prices (Nov 13) · NRCA: Construction material prices unchanged in October

GENERAL CONTRACTING
Two items
​
Demand
ABC's construction backlog indicator fell 0.6 months to 8.1 in October, 0.3 months below a year earlier, and contractors without data center work dropped from 8.3 to 7.6 months.
​
Why it matters: ABC's survey, which ran October 20 to November 3 and was released November 10, found backlog at 8.1 months, down from 8.7 in September. Contractors with data center work, most of them above $100 million in revenue, rose from 11.3 to 11.5 months. The 88% of respondents without data center work saw backlog fall from 8.3 to 7.6 months. Infrastructure backlog was 8.7 months, the lowest since May 2025. Compare your signed backlog in months with the 7.6 figure, and decide before year-end whether to hold your margin or fill January.
ABC: Construction backlog indicator drops in October
​
Labor
Research released by ABC on November 12 found multiskilled crews needed 22% to 38% fewer workdays than a modeled single-trade strategy, across 18 projects and nearly 90,000 labor hours.
​
Why it matters: The Simplar Foundation study compared actual labor records from 18 projects and 11 contractors with a modeled single-trade plan, not with observed single-trade jobs. Multiskilled crews needed about 22% to 38% fewer workdays; the same work restricted to one trade would have needed 28% to 62% more. On 73% of project days a single-trade plan would have left at least one trade short of labor. Pick the two trades you hand off between most often, cross-train one crew to cover both on your next comparable job, and time the workdays against your last one.
ABC: Multiskilling can shorten schedules by up to 38%

HOME BUILDING
Two items
​
Demand
Freddie Mac put the 30-year mortgage rate at 7.28% on November 19, up from 7.11% a week earlier and 6.26% a year ago.
​
Why it matters: Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey put the weekly average 30-year mortgage rate at 7.28% on November 19, up from 7.11% a week earlier and 6.26% a year ago. If you build spec homes, work out what a 7.28% rate does to a buyer's monthly payment and check your list prices and incentives against it before you list; if you sell to spec builders, confirm payment terms before you ship.
Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey (Nov 19)
​
Money
NAHB's third-quarter financing survey found speculative single-family construction loans at a 7.72% contract rate, up from 7.28% in the second quarter, with builders reporting net tightening for the 19th straight quarter.
​
Why it matters: NAHB's AD&C survey, posted November 13, puts builders' net credit-conditions index at -15.3, tightening for a 19th consecutive quarter. Third-quarter contract rates were 7.72% on speculative single-family construction loans and 7.27% on pre-sold construction, against 7.01% in the second quarter for pre-sold. The average effective rate on speculative loans was 11.83%, essentially unchanged. Before a spec start, get a written term sheet showing rate and points, and run your carry cost at the effective rate rather than the contract rate.
NAHB Eye on Housing: AD&C credit conditions (Nov 13)

CYBER RISK, PLAINLY
Ransomware, state hackers and AI scams: what small firms face
Criminals go where the money and customer data are. Ransomware is software that locks your files until you pay. The FBI got 3,600-plus ransomware complaints in 2025. Of 1,400-plus from firms outside critical industries, 18% were legal services firms and 17% contractors like electricians.
State-linked: US agencies assess that Iran-affiliated hackers altered equipment controllers at water, energy and local-government sites (updated July 2026). An August advisory lists commercial facilities among sectors being probed. A September fact sheet describes foreign hackers who entered an automation firm in 2025. AI scams: 22,364 FBI complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with losses over $893 million. Cloned voices can ask for wire payments.
In your trade: Contractors were 17% of ransomware complaints from firms outside critical industries; keep a tested backup of bids, plans and job files offline.
​
What to do about it: Turn on two-step sign-in wherever you can, starting with email and admins. Keep a backup on a drive not connected to your network, and test it. Confirm any change in bank details by phone, on a number you already had. If a call asks for money, hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself. Install updates. Give people only the access they need. Know who to call; report fraud at ic3.gov. The part this doesn't cover, how your systems are set up, is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report; US government advisories (updated Jul 22, Aug and Sep 23, 2026); FBI IC3 alerts; CISA and FTC guidance.
