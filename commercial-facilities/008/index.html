RULE
The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act 62-33 on November 19, setting federal environmental review deadlines and limiting agencies' power to alter issued permits.
The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act on Thursday, November 19, by 62-33, the National Asphalt Pavement Association reported November 20. The bill now goes to the House.
Its permitting provisions set clearer deadlines for federal environmental reviews, improve coordination among federal agencies, streamline Clean Water Act permitting and exempt certain activities within existing transportation rights-of-way.
The bill also limits when federal agencies can roll back, pause or significantly alter permits already issued. NAPA says permitting reform lets more road projects move from planning to construction, and its government affairs vice president said the Senate "took a historic step."
NAPA urged the House to pass the bill quickly. Its October endorsement letter asked Congress to pair permitting reform with a long-term highway bill so states have reliable funding to bid projects, and in its November 20 statement NAPA said the current highway bill expires in "just weeks" (December 11).
​
What to do about it: The bill is not law until the House passes it and it is signed, so do not price 2027 bids as if permit delays are gone. List every job waiting on a federal environmental review or Clean Water Act permit, with the agency, application date and last decision date. Put schedule-risk and price-validity language in writing on each permit-dependent bid. Ask your state DOT customers which winter and spring lettings depend on those permits. Then call your House member's office, or have your state asphalt association do it, and ask for quick passage and a long-term highway bill.

FIRE PROTECTION
Two items
​
Demand
NFSA's November 13 analysis says Florida's high-rise sprinkler retrofit requires work finished and final inspections passed by January 1, 2027, against January 1, 2033 in NFPA 101's 2024 edition.
​
Why it matters: NFSA (Nov 13) lists Florida's milestones for existing high-rise apartment buildings: engineering documents due January 1, 2025, installation permits January 1, 2026, and completed work with final inspections passed by January 1, 2027. Florida also allows a registered engineer to design an Engineered Life Safety System for certain existing condominium high-rises. Contractors with Florida backlog: confirm each customer's permit and inspection dates in writing, with the deadline 38 days away. Associations: choose a compliance method, notify the fire official and get the final-inspection date in your contract.
NFSA: Florida's High-Rise Sprinkler Retrofit Program · NFSA blog listing
​
Regulation
An NFPA tentative interim amendment would add an annual recall check to NFPA 25 and treat any recalled sprinkler as an impairment; public comment on TIA 1944 closed November 20.
​
Why it matters: NFPA's TIA 1944, submitted from the Fall River, Massachusetts fire department, would add an annual recall-status inspection to NFPA 25-2026, require recalled sprinklers to be replaced and treat systems containing them as impaired. A new Annex J lists examples, including Central, Omega, Gem, Globe and Star sprinklers. The stated aim is checking existing records, with no new testing or blanket replacement. Inspection firms: add a recall-list check to your next inspection visit and tell owners the replacement cost before it is an impairment.
NFPA 25 Proposed TIA 1944 (recalled sprinklers)

Komatsu announced its intent to acquire Astec's Roadtec and Carlson road-equipment brands, covering pavers, milling machines, material transfer vehicles and stabilizers, with closing expected January 1, 2027.
​
Why it matters: For Construction Pros reported November 12 that Komatsu plans to bring the Roadtec and Carlson brands of paving and maintenance equipment into the Komatsu family. The Maryland Asphalt Association's index page, last modified November 2, gives a prevailing monthly asphalt price of $807.50 for November 2026, the same as June through September and 31.3% above the $615.00 posted for January.

Why it matters: The Maryland Asphalt Association's index page, last modified November 2, states the prevailing price for November 2026 at $807.50. Its 2026 table shows $615.00 in January, $595.00 in February, $600.00 in March, $787.50 in April, $800.00 in May and $807.50 in each month from June through September. FACILITIES SERVICES
Two items
​
Labor
Sixteen states raise their minimum wage on January 1, 2027, including Virginia from $12.77 to $13.75 an hour and Michigan from $13.73 to $15.00, Workforce reported November 13.
​
Why it matters: Workforce's November 13 list shows Virginia up $0.98 an hour to $13.75, Washington to $17.73 from $17.13, Colorado to $15.71 from $15.16 and Michigan up $1.27 to $15.00, all effective January 1, 2027. New York's general rate does not change: $17.00 in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, $16.00 elsewhere. Janitorial, guard and food-service owners: reprice any fixed-hourly-rate contract in these states, send customers written notice before year-end, and check Maryland and D.C. rates separately.
Workforce: Minimum Wage in 2027 · Experian: 2027 state and local minimum wage changes

Regulation
OSHA's revised Hazard Communication Standard reached its November 20 employer deadline to update workplace labels, written programs and training for newly identified chemical hazards.
​
Why it matters: OSHA's January 15 final rule set November 20, 2026 as the employer date for substances: update alternative workplace labeling, the written hazard communication program and training. New labeling provisions cover containers under 100 milliliters. Mixtures come later: November 19, 2027 for manufacturers, importers and distributors and May 19, 2028 for employers. Cleaning and facilities contractors: pull your chemical list, check each safety data sheet against your program, retrain crews on any new hazard class, and keep a dated record of the training.
OSHA HazCom compliance-date extension, Federal Register · Jackson Lewis: HazCom deadlines (Sept 22)

ELEVATOR
Two items
​
Regulation
Illinois's proposed elevator rules give existing elevators without door lock monitoring until January 1, 2034 to comply; the 45-day comment period ran October 9 through November 23.
​
Why it matters: The State Fire Marshal's proposed Part 1000 rules adopt ASME A17.1-2025 and set January 1, 2034 for existing elevators without door lock monitoring; adoption is anticipated in summer 2027 and the text can still change. Elevator Blueprint, citing the Board's small business analysis, puts the cost at an estimated $10,000 to $25,000 per elevator, possibly more. Illinois owners outside Chicago: have your contractor confirm in writing, car by car, which elevators already have monitoring, and budget that range for the rest.
Illinois OSFM: proposed elevator rules 2026 (PDF) · Illinois OSFM: proposed rule changes · Elevator Blueprint: Illinois hearing and cost (Nov 3)
​
Regulation
A Miami-Dade ordinance on the November 17 county agenda would create an elevator safety code with a $150-per-unit Certificate of Operation fee and civil penalties of $250, $500 and $1,000.
​
Why it matters: A county memorandum dated November 17 for Agenda Item 4(B) would create Chapter 8F, with annual Certificates of Operation, relief for temporarily dormant equipment and each day of violation counted as a separate offense. Proposed fees include $150 per certificate renewal, $250 for an installation permit, $200 for an alteration or modernization permit and $160 an hour to witness tests. The county estimates about 10,000 certificates a year, or about $750,000 in additional annual revenue. South Florida owners: find out which authority inspects each car and calendar certificate renewals.
Miami-Dade Agenda Item 4(B): elevator safety ordinance · Elevator Blueprint: Miami-Dade and NYC rules (Nov 17)

Over Thanksgiving week a classic dinner for 10 costs $58.63, up 6%, with the 16-pound turkey at $23.68, up 10%, Farm Bureau's survey released November 18 found.

Why it matters: Farm Bureau's survey puts the dinner for 10 at $58.63, about 6% above 2025 and more than 8% below the 2022 record high; the turkey is up $2.38. Demand
U.S. restaurant and bar sales rose 0.2% in October to a seasonally adjusted $92.921 billion, up 7.0% from a year earlier, while inflation-adjusted sales fell 0.3%, Vinetur reported November 18.
​
Why it matters: Citing the Census Bureau's November 17 advance release and the National Restaurant Association's calculation, Vinetur reports October sales of $92.921 billion, up $227 million from September after a 0.9% September gain, and up $6.116 billion from October 2025. Venue and catering owners: compare your own October guest counts, not just revenue, with last year before you set 2027 budgets and menu prices.

CYBER RISK, PLAINLY
Ransomware, state hackers and AI scams: what small firms face
Criminals go where the money and customer data are. Ransomware is software that locks your files until you pay. The FBI got 3,600-plus ransomware complaints in 2025. Of 1,400-plus from firms outside critical industries, 18% were legal services firms and 17% contractors like electricians.
State-linked: US agencies assess that Iran-affiliated hackers altered equipment controllers at water, energy and local-government sites (updated July 2026). An August advisory lists commercial facilities among sectors being probed. A September fact sheet describes foreign hackers who entered an automation firm in 2025. AI scams: 22,364 FBI complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with losses over $893 million. Cloned voices can ask for wire payments.
In your trade: A September 2026 federal fact sheet on industrial control systems warns of supply chain risk; ask vendors that connect to your systems how and when.
​
What to do about it: Turn on two-step sign-in wherever you can, starting with email and admins. Keep a backup on a drive not connected to your network, and test it. Confirm any change in bank details by phone, on a number you already had. If a call asks for money, hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself. Install updates. Give people only the access they need. Know who to call; report fraud at ic3.gov. The part this doesn't cover, how your systems are set up, is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report; US government advisories (updated Jul 22, Aug and Sep 23, 2026); FBI IC3 alerts; CISA and FTC guidance.
