A 50% additional duty on 554 lines of Canadian goods — cement, plastics, adhesives, machinery, electrical equipment and carpet among them — took effect August 22.
A 50% additional ad valorem duty on Canadian goods took effect at 12:01 a.m. on August 22, 2026, under three Section 338 proclamations signed July 20 and published July 23. Customs and Border Protection issued entry guidance the night before, in CSMS message 69606660, directing filers to new Chapter 99 headings 9903.03.12 through 9903.03.14 for covered goods. The duty stacks on top of ordinary customs duties, other trade remedies and antidumping or countervailing duties. USMCA origin does not exempt a covered good.
The proclamations are named for dairy, alcohol and motor vehicles, but the annexes run to roughly 554 eight-digit subheadings. Coverage reported by customs advisers includes cement, plastics and plastic fittings and floor coverings, adhesives and waxes, machinery, pumps, hand tools, electrical and telecommunications equipment, printed circuit boards, textiles, yarns and carpets. Heading 9903.03.15 carries a 0% rate for steel, aluminum and copper articles, vehicles, wood, semiconductors and patented pharmaceuticals, which sit under Section 232 instead.
The date moved twice. The original effective date was August 19. A proclamation signed August 18 suspended the duties for three days while negotiations continued, and CBP refunded anything collected in that window. No deal followed, so collection began August 22. Energy products, potash, fish and certain critical minerals are excluded, as are qualifying civil aircraft articles. Goods entering a foreign trade zone must be admitted under privileged foreign status, not domestic status.
Canada has published its answer. Countermeasures on about $27.6 billion in imports from the U.S. take effect at 12:01 a.m. on September 8, 2026, at 15%, 25% and 50% rates set to match the U.S. rate on the same goods, covering steel products, appliances, agricultural equipment, electronics, pulp and paper, textiles and wood. Goods already in transit are exempt. On August 25 the National Fire Sprinkler Association asked the administration to lift the Canadian tariffs on fire pumps and their controllers.
​
What to do about it: Pull every open purchase order and quote with a Canadian country of origin and get the eight-digit HTS classification from the supplier in writing this week — the proclamation annexes, not the product description, decide whether you owe 50%. Have your customs broker confirm whether each line falls under 9903.03.12 through .14 at 50% or 9903.03.15 and .16 at 0%; a USMCA certificate does not help here. Reprice any fixed-price bid still open that carries Canadian cement, plastic pipe and fittings, adhesives, carpet, hand tools, electrical gear or pumps, and cut bid validity to 14 to 30 days. Put a tariff-escalation clause in every new agreement that names the specific duty program rather than saying "tariffs." If you ship anything north, check September and October deliveries against Canada's September 8 list.
CSMS #69606660 - Guidance: Section 338 Additional Duties on Certain Goods of Canada (U.S. Customs and Border Protection, August 21, 2026)  ·  Proclamation 11048, Imposing Additional Duties To Offset Canadian Discrimination Against the Commerce of the United States With Respect to Motor Vehicles (Federal Register, July 23, 2026)  ·  List of products from the United States subject to counter-tariffs effective September 8, 2026 (Department of Finance Canada)

FIRE PROTECTION
Two items
​
Supply
The National Fire Sprinkler Association asked the administration on August 25 to lift Canadian tariffs on fire pumps and controllers, citing escalation risk up to 50%
​
Why it matters: NFSA asked the administration on August 25, 2026 to lift tariffs on Canadian fire and life safety products, naming fire pump controllers — the "brains" of a fire pump — and the makers its members buy from: Eaton, Tornatech, Peerless Pumps, SPP Pumps and Armstrong Pumps. It cites escalation risk "up to 50%" and tariffs "issued this week," matching the 50% Section 338 duty that began August 22. This is a request, not relief; no exclusion process has been announced. Contractors with fire pump packages on order or quoted are exposed; sprinkler pipe is not, because steel articles sit in the 0% carve-out. Call your pump vendor this week for country of origin and the HTS code on every controller in your backlog, and re-confirm quoted pump prices in writing before you sign.
National Fire Sprinkler Association Urges Tariff Relief on Fire & Life Safety Components (GlobeNewswire, August 25, 2026)  ·  CSMS #69606660 - Guidance: Section 338 Additional Duties on Certain Goods of Canada (U.S. Customs and Border Protection, August 21, 2026)

Regulation
The 2026 edition of NFPA 25 moves hose valve inspections from annual to quarterly, adds a quarterly solenoid test, and requires annual internal inspection of preaction and deluge valves
​
Why it matters: The 2026 edition of NFPA 25 changes the inspection frequencies your service contracts are priced on. Hose valve inspections move from annual to quarterly. Preaction and deluge valves need an annual internal inspection, ending the five-year allowance for resettable designs. Solenoid coils on electric actuators get a quarterly supervisory test. The hydrostatic-test exemption for short fire department connection piping widens from 4 to 10 feet. Dwelling-unit sprinklers installed 50 years ago must be replaced with fast-response heads or sample-tested. A frozen system is now impaired, not deficient. Adoption is jurisdiction by jurisdiction. Ask your fire marshal which edition is enforced, then price quarterly hose valve visits into your contract before renewal.
What's Changing in NFPA 25: Key Updates from the 2026 Edition (International Fire Suppression Alliance, April 7, 2026)  ·  What the 2026 Edition of NFPA 25 Means for Fire Sprinkler Inspections (Chesapeake Sprinkler Company, July 24, 2026)

PAVING
Two items
​
Supply
Missouri's posted asphalt cement price index rose to $631.25 for August 2026 from the $605.00 it held in June and July, and is up 29.8% since January
​
Why it matters: MoDOT's monthly asphalt price index, a common private escalation reference, posted $631.25 for August 2026 — up 4.3% from the $605.00 it held in June and July, and 29.8% above January's $486.25. The 2026 series runs $486.25 in January, $481.25 in February, $497.50 in March, $585.00 in April and May, $605.00 in June and July, and $631.25 in August. That is the fourth increase of 2026 and the first move since April. DOT contracts with an asphalt cement adjustment clause pass it through; fixed-price private paving, parking lot and sealcoat work absorbs all of it. MoDOT publishes no unit, so read it as a percentage move, not a per-ton price. Reprice every private quote written before August, and tie new agreements to your state DOT's published monthly index.

Labor
The Service Contract Act health and welfare fringe rate rose to $5.92 an hour on August 10, 2026, from $5.55, a 37-cent increase on every hour worked
​
Why it matters: The Labor Department's All Agency Memorandum 252, dated August 10, 2026, set the Service Contract Act health and welfare fringe at $5.92 an hour, up from $5.55. The Executive Order 13706 paid-sick-leave rate rises to $5.42 from $5.09, and Hawaii's rates are $2.51 and $2.01. On a 2,080-hour year, 37 cents is roughly $770 per full-time employee before payroll taxes. It hits janitorial, grounds, O&M and guard services under federal contracts, but does not bite until a wage determination carrying the rate is incorporated into your contract, so exposure lands at option-year exercise or modification. Check every federal contract for a price-adjustment clause now. DC's security officer minimum wage fringe tracks the federal Guard 1 rate, so the District's number moves next.

SECURITY SYSTEMS
Two items
​
Regulation
The FCC published a proposal on August 7, 2026 to bar equipment authorization for any device containing a Covered List component, with comments due September 8
​
Why it matters: The FCC adopted a Third Report and Order and Third Further Notice in ET Docket 21-232 on July 22, 2026, releasing them July 23. The order bars equipment authorization for devices with logic-bearing hardware from Covered List entities — Hikvision and Dahua video surveillance gear among them — effective 30 days after Federal Register publication. It is prospective only, leaves previously authorized equipment alone, and exempts applications pending on the effective date. The Further Notice, published August 7, 2026, asks whether to extend the bar to any Covered List component or adopt a rebuttable presumption against authorization. Comments are due September 8, replies September 21. Ask each camera and intercom manufacturer in writing which lines are affected.
Protecting Against National Security Threats to the Communications Supply Chain through the Equipment Authorization Program, proposed rule (Federal Register, August 7, 2026)  ·  FCC 26-50, Third Report and Order and Third Further Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, ET Docket 21-232 (Federal Communications Commission, released July 23, 2026)

Supply
Forced-labor Section 301 tariffs of 10% to 12.5% took effect July 24, 2026 across 60 economies, and the security hardware exclusions the industry asked for were not granted
​
Why it matters: USTR's forced-labor Section 301 final action hits products entered for consumption on or after 12:01 a.m. July 24, 2026, with a grace period for cargo loaded earlier and entered by July 28. Seventeen economies including Canada, Mexico, India and the UK draw 10% stacked on existing rates; the EU and Taiwan pay 10% net of MFN; Japan, Korea and Switzerland pay 12.5% net; everyone else, China and Vietnam included, pays 12.5% stacked. It reaches 60 trading partners and more than 99% of U.S. imports. Section 232 goods, USMCA-compliant products, civil aircraft and pharmaceuticals are excluded. SIA sought exclusions for 47 subheadings covering security components on July 6; the published exclusion lists do not include them. Requote cameras, readers and intercoms at landed cost.

ELEVATOR
Two items
​
Demand
Otis reported modernization backlog up 24% and new equipment orders down 5% for the second quarter of 2026 — elevator money has moved to buildings already standing
​
Why it matters: Otis's second-quarter 2026 results, released July 22, 2026, show where elevator revenue is. Modernization organic sales and backlog each rose 24%, backlog 26% at constant currency, with orders up 9%. Maintenance and repair organic sales rose 6%. New equipment went the other way: organic sales down 1% and orders down 5% at constant currency, with a high-teens decline in China and a mid-single-digit decline in EMEA offsetting roughly 10% organic growth in the Americas. Service net sales reached $2.6 billion, up 11%, against $1.3 billion and flat in new equipment. For an independent service contractor, that argues for modernization proposals and route density, not new-construction bids. Price modernization work off current labor and material rates, not last year's.
Otis Reports Second Quarter 2026 Results (Otis Worldwide, July 22, 2026)  ·  Otis Reports Second Quarter 2026 Results (PR Newswire, July 22, 2026)

Regulation
Florida adopted ASME A17.3-2020 for existing elevators on May 25, 2026, moving the Part 3.10.12 compliance date to August 1, 2025 but deferring enforcement to August 1, 2029
​
Why it matters: Florida's Bureau of Elevator Safety adopted Rule 61C-5.001 on May 25, 2026, incorporating ASME A17.1-2019 for elevators and escalators, A17.3-2020 for existing ones, and A18.1-2020 for platform lifts and chairlifts, aligning with the ninth edition of the Florida Building Code. It moves the Part 3.10.12 effective date from December 31, 2023 to August 1, 2025 — existing elevators must comply — but bars enforcement until August 1, 2029. Four requirements were left out: private residence elevators, emergency brakes at 3.8.5, ascending car overspeed at 3.10.13 and 3.13.1, and unintended car movement at 3.10.14 and 3.13.2. A17.3 is the existing-installation code: retrofit work on buildings in service. Quote it against the 2029 date now, not as a change order later.
Bureau of Elevator Safety News Room, Adoption of 61C-5.001, F.A.C., May 25, 2026 (Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation)

Insurance
Liquor liability premiums have risen 300% to 600% in some states, with small bars moving from about $5,000 a year to $50,000 or more regardless of claims history
​
Why it matters: An August 3, 2026 review in the magazine of the Independent Insurance Agents and Brokers of America reports liquor liability premiums up 300% to 600% in some states, with small bars going from about $5,000 a year to $50,000 or more regardless of loss history, as carriers withdraw capacity over claims and outsized verdicts. Forty-three states have a dram shop statute; seven do not — Delaware, Kansas, Louisiana, Maryland, Nevada, South Dakota and Virginia. Read the indemnity and additional-insured language in every venue agreement you sign this fall, and get your liquor liability renewal quoted 90 days out.

THE BACKGROUND SIGNAL
The fraud that reads your invoices before it sends one
The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center took 24,768 business email compromise reports in 2025, with losses of $3.05 billion — a record, after dipping to $2.77 billion in 2024 from $2.95 billion in 2023. Across all internet crime it logged 1,008,597 complaints and $20.88 billion in losses. Reported cases only, so read it as a floor.
Business email compromise, not ransomware, is the one that should concern a commercial contractor. Someone gets into a mailbox, reads until they understand how you invoice and who pays you, then sends a real-looking progress billing with different bank details. No malware, no warning — just a payment that never arrives and an argument about who eats it. A trade that emails proposals, service tickets and supplier invoices all day is the shape this is built for. The quieter number is adoption: under 20% of firms with fewer than 20 staff use AI at all.
​
What to do about it: You don't need a security program. You need multi-factor authentication on email and your accounting login, and one rule everybody follows: nobody changes bank details on the strength of an email or a call — you ring the number you already had on file and confirm. That covers most of it, and costs nothing. The part it doesn't — how the systems are set up — is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
