TRADE
Compass sued 15 MLSs on November 12 over fines of up to $15,000 for publicly marketing listings, and a New York judge told it to file its motions by December 3.
Compass filed suits against 15 MLSs on November 12, alleging that rules fining agents who publicly market homes violate federal antitrust law, RISMedia reported. The 45-page Hive MLS complaint alleges conspiracy and an illegal group boycott. Compass says the fines reach up to $15,000.
Compass says seven of the top 25 MLSs, covering 340,000 agents in 12 states, allow office exclusives without fines. The MLSs call their rules pro-competitive. Nine of them already sued Compass in New York across two suits filed October 5 and October 21 and amended since, seeking rulings that the rules are lawful.
On November 13 Compass wrote the court asking that the New York action be dismissed or split so each MLS's dispute is heard in its home district. On November 19 Judge Mary Kay Vyskocil denied its request for a pre-motion conference and left the schedule alone, so Compass's motions are due December 3, Real Estate News reported.
Compass argues each case turns on local rules and local sales prices. Arizona's MLS told the court on November 18 that splitting the cases would cause "massive inefficiencies and duplication of effort." CRMLS and the Council of Multiple Listing Services are each working on a legal defense fund.
​
What to do about it: Pull your MLS's current rules on public marketing and office exclusives, and note its fine schedule. The suits name 15 MLSs, so find out whether yours is a defendant or a plaintiff. Ask your managing broker or counsel in writing what your office owes if an agent is fined, and whether any listing agreement lets an agent market publicly before MLS submission. If Compass competes in your market, ask your MLS whether its enforcement changed after November 12. Brief agents before the holiday week on what the rules allow, keep every client marketing instruction in writing, and check the docket again after December 3.

PROPERTY MANAGEMENT
Two items
​
Regulation
HUD's final rule requiring status verification for everyone in covered assisted housing takes effect December 14, and owners of project-based Section 8 and Section 236 units must run the checks.
​
Why it matters: HUD finalized the rule November 12, with Federal Register publication scheduled that day. Owners of project-based Section 8, Section 236 and Rent Supplement units must verify status through DHS's SAVE system for every household member of any age; housing agencies handle vouchers. Certain tenants must be notified by January 11, 2027, and mixed-status families have 90 days, to March 14, 2027, to submit evidence, Ballard Spahr says. Pull your assisted-unit rent roll, flag households without documentation, and update notices and consent forms before December 14.
NAA: HUD finalizes status verification rule · Federal Register public inspection: HUD final rule · Ballard Spahr: HUD final rule key deadlines
​
Demand
NAA's November 14 analysis of CoStar data for 394 metros found effective rents fell an average 4.8% from 2022 to 2025 in the 76 markets that added more than 20% to apartment inventory.
​
Why it matters: In the 108 markets that grew inventory by less than 4%, rents rose an average 7.8% over the same years, and 47 of the 394 markets cut rents outright. NAHB projects multifamily starts will fall 5% in 2026 and 6% in 2027, after 336,000 in 2024 and 397,000 in 2025. A new lease took about 36% of typical renter income in late 2025, against about 39% in early 2022. Count the units under construction near each of your buildings, and price December and January renewals against those new properties' asking rents.
NAA: new apartment supply delivered rent relief

INDEPENDENT PRACTICES
Two items
​
Labor
MGMA's November 10 poll found 72% of medical practices plan 1% to 3% staff raises for 2027, 18% plan 4% to 6%, 9% plan none and 1% plan 7% or more.
​
Why it matters: The share planning 4% or more fell to 18% from 24% in MGMA's September 2025 poll for 2026 budgets, MGMA reported November 11 from 265 responses. It also cites MGMA's 2026 DataDive report, which found 56% of practices said hiring medical assistants had become harder over the past year and 7% easier. DataDive medians show 2025 total pay up 4.8% for medical receptionists and 4.3% for medical assistants. Set your 2027 raise budget against these bands now, and price a retention adjustment for front-desk and medical-assistant roles before January.
MGMA Stat: staff pay in 2027 tightens
​
Money
The No Surprises Act dispute Gateway began accepting filings from account holders on November 13, and standalone web forms disappear after January 15, 2027, except the resubmission form.
​
Why it matters: Batched disputes with open negotiation periods starting on or after November 1 are limited to 50 line items, with a 30-business-day cooling-off period, Holland & Knight says. Any organization that files or represents a party needs its own Gateway account, so a practice using an outside billing vendor should confirm the vendor has one. The administrative fee is $15 per party, down from $115, Holland & Knight says, and the resubmission form is the one web form that stays after January 15. Ask your billing vendor this week whether its account is live, and move open disputes onto the batching rules before the January 15 cutoff.
AMA: Nov. 20, 2026 national advocacy update · Holland & Knight: federal IDR operational updates · CMS: Federal IDR operations implementation timeline

INDEPENDENT AGENCIES
Two items
​
Insurance
Ivans reported November 10 that October commercial renewal increases eased on auto, business owners, liability and umbrella, rose to 5.37% on property and fell 1.45% on workers' compensation.
​
Why it matters: The Ivans Index, built on more than 120 million transactions from 38,000-plus agencies, put October renewal changes at 6.08% for umbrella, 5.21% for business owners policies, 3.10% for general liability and 2.80% for commercial auto. Commercial auto averaged 3.63% in the third quarter against 7.6% a year earlier, and commercial property 5.65% against 7.64%. Pull your own October renewals by line, compare them with these averages, and call accounts renewing within 90 days with line-level numbers before the carrier quote arrives.
Insurance Journal: Ivans, renewal increases slowed in Oct.
​
Demand
Aon closed its $17.0 billion purchase of USI Insurance Services on November 16, while independent agencies wrote 87.7% of commercial lines premium in 2025, Insurance Business reported.
​
Why it matters: USI now operates as USI, an Aon company, in a combined middle-market business of about $6.5 billion in annual revenue. Independents placed 62% of all U.S. property-casualty premium in 2025, up from 61.5%, per IIABA data the article cites. It calls producer and client retention the most common failure point in deals of this size. If you hold middle-market accounts, review producer agreements for non-solicit and servicing terms, and call your ten largest clients this month.
Insurance Business: Aon closes USI deal

SMALL ACCOUNTING PRACTICES
Two items
​
Regulation
Treasury and the IRS finalized automatic-enrollment rules for 401(k) and 403(b) plans on November 17, starting at a 3% default and rising at least 1 point a year to at least 10%.
​
Why it matters: The final regulations, T.D. 10061, generally apply to plan years beginning after December 31, 2027. The IRS says they clarify exceptions for qualifying small and new businesses and SIMPLE 401(k) plans. The default is capped at 15%, and employees can opt out. Treasury estimates about $4.2 billion a year of added savings over 2023 to 2032. Check whether your firm's own plan is exempt, and tell clients that sponsor plans to read the exception text before 2028 plan years.
IRS: Treasury, IRS issue final retirement rules · JofA: IRS finalizes auto-enrollment rules
​
Regulation
Practitioners are reporting a new round of CP53E notices issued after the October 15 deadline that may refund 2025 overpayments clients meant to credit to 2026, Journal of Accountancy reported November 23.
​
Why it matters: CP53E notices are issued when the IRS changes how an overpayment is applied. If a refund replaces the credit, a client can face underpayment penalties and return mismatches. The AICPA raised the issue at a November 12 town hall and had not received an IRS response, and could not tell whether the notices were issued in error. A September TIGTA report found the CP53E rollout was hampered by limited communication. List clients who elected to apply 2025 overpayments to 2026, and check each IRS account transcript before the next estimated payment.
Journal of Accountancy: AICPA seeks answers on CP53E

STAFFING AGENCIES
Two items
​
Demand
SIA-Bullhorn put U.S. staffing hours 11% above a year earlier for the week ending November 7, at an indexed 109, just under the 110 of October 31, with Thanksgiving ahead.
​
Why it matters: Bullhorn's note says the last full week before Thanksgiving is normally the annual peak for staffing hours, and IT hours dipped in the usual pre-Thanksgiving pattern. Commercial hours rose 13% on the year to a record, light industrial 18% and professional 9%, while office and clerical fell 8%. The indicator was 106 in the week ending September 26. SIA says the readings for the last four weeks are preliminary. Over Thanksgiving week, hours and billing can move fast: confirm holiday-pay and billing terms with each client in writing before Thursday, and check that your payroll cut-off dates match the holiday schedule.
SIA: SIA-Bullhorn Staffing Indicator, Nov. 17, 2026 · Bullhorn: Staffing Industry Indicator
​
Money
California missed the November 10 deadline to repay its federal unemployment loan, so employers there face a 1.5-point FUTA credit reduction on 2026 wages, due with Form 940 by February 1, 2027.
​
Why it matters: The credit falls from 5.4% to 3.9%, a net FUTA rate of 2.1%, or $147 per employee earning $7,000 or more against $42 elsewhere, an extra $105, the American Staffing Association reported November 19. The Labor Department's final list, revised November 10, shows California with a 1.5-point reduction; the Virgin Islands repaid and shows 0.0%, the American Payroll Association said November 13. Staffing firms with California payrolls should accrue the extra $105 per qualifying worker now and reprice open California bill rates before year-end.
ASA: California employers face higher FUTA bill · APA: California FUTA credit reduction

BROKERAGES AND AGENT TEAMS
Two items
​
Regulation
New York Gov. Hochul signed a law requiring timely public marketing of residential listings, with a seller opt-out through a state disclosure form, RISMedia reported November 23.
​
Why it matters: Sellers who opt out must acknowledge and sign a state-required disclosure form, NYSAR's Mike Kelly said. Connecticut's governor signed a similar law in May 2026 that also includes an opt-out. Pull your office's private-listing and office-exclusive forms, and ask your state association for the effective date and the disclosure form before you market any New York listing privately.
RISMedia: NY private-listings bill becomes law
​
Money
Federal funding runs out December 11, and NerdWallet reported November 13 that a lapse would stop IRS income and Social Security number verification, which could delay some mortgages.
​
Why it matters: H.R. 6500, which Congress approved in early September, funds operations through December 11, The Tax Adviser reported September 3. As of NerdWallet's November 13 report, leaders in both parties backed a short-term extension, while some Senate Democrats threatened to withhold support. A lapse could also delay tax refunds. Ask your lenders now which buyers in your December pipeline need IRS income verification, and where you control contract dates, set closings ahead of December 11.
NerdWallet: lame-duck Congress and shutdown · The Tax Adviser: Congress approves funding bill

CYBER RISK, PLAINLY
Ransomware, state hackers and AI scams: what small firms face
Criminals go where the money and customer data are. Ransomware is software that locks your files until you pay. The FBI got 3,600-plus ransomware complaints in 2025. Of 1,400-plus from firms outside critical industries, 18% were legal services firms and 17% contractors like electricians.
State-linked: US agencies assess that Iran-affiliated hackers altered equipment controllers at water, energy and local-government sites (updated July 2026). An August advisory lists commercial facilities among sectors being probed. A September fact sheet describes foreign hackers who entered an automation firm in 2025. AI scams: 22,364 FBI complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with losses over $893 million. Cloned voices can ask for wire payments.
In your trade: Legal services firms were 18% and engineering and architectural firms 10% of ransomware complaints outside critical industries; back up files offline.
​
What to do about it: Turn on two-step sign-in wherever you can, starting with email and admins. Keep a backup on a drive not connected to your network, and test it. Confirm any change in bank details by phone, on a number you already had. If a call asks for money, hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself. Install updates. Give people only the access they need. Know who to call; report fraud at ic3.gov. The part this doesn't cover, how your systems are set up, is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report; US government advisories (updated Jul 22, Aug and Sep 23, 2026); FBI IC3 alerts; CISA and FTC guidance.
