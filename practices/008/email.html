RULE
HHS says it issued a final HIPAA Privacy Rule on November 16 that cuts the time to answer patient records requests from 30 days to 15; ADA News reports compliance is due July 19, 2027.
HHS's Office for Civil Rights says it issued a final rule revising the HIPAA Privacy Rule on November 16, effective 60 days after publication, with compliance 180 days after the effective date. ADA News reports Federal Register publication on November 19, an effective date of January 19, 2027 and a compliance date of July 19, 2027, with extra transition time for certain existing business associate agreements. The ADA's summary is written for dental practices; any other covered provider should confirm the same changes with its own counsel.
Under the rule, a practice must answer a patient's request for records within 15 calendar days, down from 30. One 30-day extension remains, but the practice must give written notice within the first 15 days stating the reason and the date it will respond. Practices that charge for copies must make their fee schedule available to individuals; those with websites must post it online and provide it at the point of service and on request.
Electronic records must be provided at no charge in certain circumstances, and in-person inspection and qualifying automated internet-based access must be free. Notices of privacy practices must be updated for new content requirements, but practices no longer need patients' written acknowledgment that they received the notice. Identity checks must be reasonable, and stricter state privacy rules or shorter state release deadlines still apply.
HHS did not finalize rights to inspect, photograph or record protected health information during appointments, individualized fee estimates and itemized bills, or broader minimum-necessary exceptions for care coordination. The ADA's resource lists records a request can reach, including CBCT images, digital impressions and surgical planning files, even files a vendor keeps for the practice, and includes an implementation checklist.
​
What to do about it: Confirm the effective and compliance dates in the Federal Register text, because HHS and the ADA state them differently. Name one privacy official this week and give that person ownership of every records request. Start a request log with the date received and an alert on day 12, because the 30-day extension needs written notice inside the first 15 days. List where each record type lives, including imaging and vendor-hosted files, and ask each vendor in writing how it will produce them on a 15-day clock. Price your copy fees against the new fee-schedule requirement and draft the website posting well before the compliance date. Run one mock request in the first quarter of 2027, and check whether your state's release deadline is shorter.
ADA News: Final HIPAA Privacy Rule and records access · HHS OCR: 2026 HIPAA Privacy Final Rule

DENTAL
Two items
​
Insurance
The ADA said November 16 that dentists keep reporting MetLife payment offsets to recover overpayments made to other, unrelated offices; MetLife calls the cases isolated processing errors.
​
Why it matters: MetLife told the ADA in 2025 it would offset overpayments only against the provider that received them, with systems changes due by the end of 2025, yet members still report reduced payments tied to other offices. The ADA asked MetLife on October 19 whether the change is fully in place; MetLife says it does not offset across unrelated providers and that the cited cases were corrected. If a MetLife remittance comes in short, ask MetLife for the reason, the provider linked to the overpayment, the recovery method and any related claim adjustments, and report it to dentalbenefits@ada.org.
ADA News: MetLife offsetting payments owed to dentists
​
Money
Reps. Blake Moore and Mike Thompson introduced the Dental Freedom and Tax Savings Act on November 20 to make fixed-fee in-office dental plans payable with FSA and HSA funds.
​
Why it matters: The bill would clarify that a fixed periodic fee for access to dental services, with no insurance underwriting or risk pooling, qualifies for FSA and HSA spending, and would let employers reimburse employees for it tax-free without buying group dental insurance. It has only been introduced. The ADA reports that 31.5% of practices already offer in-office plans and 16.9% are considering them. If you run one, confirm your plan paperwork shows a fixed periodic fee and no insurance risk, the arrangement the bill describes.
ADA News: FSA/HSA bill for dental service arrangements

VETERINARY
Two items
​
Supply
The FTC and 13 state attorneys general sued November 13 in federal court in New York to block Covetrus's $3.5 billion purchase of MWI Veterinary Supply, the two largest distributors to clinics.
​
Why it matters: The complaint says the two firms are each other's closest competitors in distribution to veterinary clinics and to large corporate accounts, and that discounts, rebates and the Vetcove price-comparison platform produced an "all-out price war" that smaller distributors cannot match. The FTC alleges the merged firm would raise clinics' costs; Covetrus says clinics buy from several suppliers and the deal would improve practice economics. Pull your 12-month purchases by distributor and your current rebate terms, and get a written quote from a second supplier before any contract renewal.
FTC: Sues to block Covetrus acquisition of MWI · Minnesota AG: Covetrus merger challenge (Nov 16) · dvm360: FTC sues to block Covetrus-MWI merger
​
Demand
Arizona's November 9 memo says dogs and cats leaving a New World screwworm zone for another state need an Arizona premovement permit and an electronic health certificate, and pets moving within Arizona from a zone need an approved screwworm treatment.
​
Why it matters: Any client leaving Arizona with a pet needs an appointment with a USDA-accredited veterinarian for a certificate of veterinary inspection. Leaving Arizona from an infested zone requires an Arizona premovement permit and an electronic certificate, plus the destination state's requirements; pets moving within Arizona need an approved or emergency-authorized screwworm treatment. Three 20 km zones are active, and confirmed cases are two dogs, three horses and one sheep. dvm360 advises reviewing destination requirements well before departure. Hold certificate slots this week, call the destination state's animal health office before issuing, and report maggot-infested wounds to AZDA at 602-542-4293.
dvm360: Arizona dog and cat travel guidance, screwworm

AESTHETICS
Two items
​
Regulation
Kentucky's Board of Pharmacy voted November 18 to enforce its compounding rule as written and refuse waivers for the six peptides HHS said it would not pursue federally, including pharmacies shipping in from other states.
​
Why it matters: The board's November 19 compliance alert says none of BPC-157, TB-500, Epitalon, Semax, KPV or MOTS-c meets the requirements of 201 KAR 2:076, so pharmacies, including nonresident ones shipping into Kentucky, may not compound, dispense or ship them. HHS's interim policy does not approve the peptides, add them to FDA's 503A bulks list or override state law, and the AMA asked HHS on November 11 to withdraw it. Before you stock or sell any of the six, get written confirmation from your compounding pharmacy and your state boards that state law allows it.
AmSpa: Kentucky rules on six peptides · AmSpa: AMA urges HHS to withdraw peptide proposal
​
Regulation
State regulators in New Jersey, West Virginia, Virginia, New Mexico and Alabama have issued guidance on medical spas, IV therapy, GLP-1 prescribing and compounding, AmSpa's general counsel wrote November 19.
​
Why it matters: New Jersey's multi-board circular bars selecting prescription ingredients from an IV menu and says standing orders cannot replace individualized evaluations. West Virginia's nursing board treats adding a drug, vitamin, mineral or amino acid to an IV solution as compounding. Virginia's guidance covers patient evaluation, staff roles and compounding, including adding substances to saline bags. New Mexico approved GLP-1 prescribing guidance. Arkansas's IV and aesthetics statements are still proposals. Compare your IV menu, standing orders and medical director agreement against your state's guidance this week.
AmSpa: State boards paying more attention to med spas

CHIROPRACTIC & PHYSICAL THERAPY
Two items
​
Labor
APTA's fourth benchmark report on private-practice outpatient physical therapy, released November 18, found practices grew their workforce 11% in 2026 while job openings rose nearly 25%.
​
Why it matters: The report, from APTA and APTA Private Practice, uses 2025 and 2026 survey data, and APTA's headline says demand for PTs and PTAs is outpacing workforce growth. Practices added staff at 11%, but openings rose by nearly 25%. Before year-end, list every open clinical position with days open and the pay offered, so you can compare your own hiring pace against the full report.
APTA: Demand for PTs and PTAs outpacing workforce
​
Money
APTA said November 10 that the final 2027 Medicare fee schedule has a mixed payment impact for physical therapy, and MedBridge lists remote therapeutic monitoring device codes falling from $51.44 to $40.70.
​
Why it matters: APTA's summary says the final rule's practice expense changes should improve the value of many PT services over time and that top-billed PT codes secured yearly payment increases for the next several years. MedBridge lists device codes 98977 and 98985 falling about 20%, from $51.44 to $40.70, and set-up code 98975 from $21.70 to $19.70. Nixon Peabody's November 9 alert says CMS gave 98977, 98978 and 98986 the same practice expense value, that revaluations cutting total RVUs by 20% or more must phase in over at least two years, and that the rule gives no detail on reimbursement rates. If you bill RTM, check your contractor's 2027 fee file before you model January revenue.
APTA: Final 2027 fee schedule, mixed impact for PT · MedBridge: 2027 final rule, RTM and rehab payment · Nixon Peabody: CMS finalizes 2027 RPM and RTM changes

OPTOMETRY & VISION
Two items
​
Demand
Tenpoint Therapeutics launched a telehealth program on November 9 that lets adults with presbyopia get Yuvezzi after a remote assessment by a licensed optometrist or ophthalmologist, with the drug shipped to the patient.
​
Why it matters: Patients enroll at orderYUVEZZI.com or by phone, and a licensed eye care provider decides whether the drop is appropriate. Tenpoint calls the program a complement, not a replacement, for in-office care and launched YUVEZZIdoctor.com, a directory of local eye care professionals, Optometry Times reported November 10. Optometrists can prescribe Yuvezzi within state scope, and some patients may start it before an exam. At routine visits, ask every presbyopia patient about drop use and review low-light vision and retinal-symptom counseling.
Optometry Times: Yuvezzi telehealth for presbyopia
​
Demand
The Vision Council's third-quarter survey, reported November 9, found managed vision care coverage at a five-quarter high of 72% and more shoppers spending $100 to $199 on eyewear.
​
Why it matters: The survey of 12,099 U.S. adults, fielded July 29 to September 12, found 59% of contact lens buyers spent under $100 out of pocket, the highest share in five quarters. Among respondents without a recent eyewear purchase, 37% cited stretching their current pair, up from 35%. Online eyewear buying was about 15%; about half of past online buyers returned to buy in person against 7% who moved the other way. Online buyers were likelier to buy multiple pairs (53%) than in-person buyers (36%). Offer a second-pair price at the dispensing table.
Optometry Times: Vision Council Q3 eyewear spending

BEHAVIORAL & MENTAL HEALTH
Two items
​
Regulation
CMS told states on November 13 they may pause Medicaid work-requirement denials and disenrollments through June 30, 2027, and states wanting a January 1 start must notify CMS by December 11.
​
Why it matters: Under SMD# 26-006, a state with an approved hold must still assess every application and renewal for compliance or exclusion, but CMS will not take enforcement action or count those eligibility decisions as errors in audits; the pause does not reach age, income or other criteria. KFF reports CMS says verifying medical frailty does not require proof that a condition limits ability to work, following a recent court ruling. Ask your state Medicaid agency and managed care plans whether they will request the hold, and plan your January Medicaid caseload around the answer.
CMS: SMD# 26-006 hold harmless letter (Nov 13) · KFF: Optional 6-month delay on Medicaid work rules
​
Money
CMS's November 4 fact sheet says 2027 is the final year of the four-year transition of the work-RVU increase for timed behavioral health codes, including psychotherapy, and waives the in-person mental health visit requirement through 2027.
​
Why it matters: CMS says the adjustment began under the CY 2024 rule and runs over a four-year transition; smoking and tobacco cessation and SBIRT services join its final year. The fact sheet, dated November 4, also says the in-person visit requirement for mental health visits is waived for services furnished through December 31, 2027, citing the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026. Load the 2027 RVUs for your psychotherapy and screening codes into your charge master before January 1, and ask each commercial payer in writing whether it follows Medicare's in-person rule.
CMS: CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule fact sheet

CYBER RISK, PLAINLY
Ransomware, state hackers and AI scams: what small firms face
Criminals go where the money and customer data are. Ransomware is software that locks your files until you pay. The FBI got 3,600-plus ransomware complaints in 2025. Of 1,400-plus from firms outside critical industries, 18% were legal services firms and 17% contractors like electricians.
State-linked: US agencies assess that Iran-affiliated hackers altered equipment controllers at water, energy and local-government sites (updated July 2026). An August advisory lists commercial facilities among sectors being probed. A September fact sheet describes foreign hackers who entered an automation firm in 2025. AI scams: 22,364 FBI complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with losses over $893 million. Cloned voices can ask for wire payments.
In your trade: An August 2026 federal advisory calls healthcare a frequent victim of Medusa ransomware; keep a tested backup that is not online.
​
What to do about it: Turn on two-step sign-in wherever you can, starting with email and admins. Keep a backup on a drive not connected to your network, and test it. Confirm any change in bank details by phone, on a number you already had. If a call asks for money, hang up and call back on a number you look up yourself. Install updates. Give people only the access they need. Know who to call; report fraud at ic3.gov. The part this doesn't cover, how your systems are set up, is the day job of our parent company, CyberSainya.
FBI IC3 2025 Internet Crime Report; US government advisories (updated Jul 22, Aug and Sep 23, 2026); FBI IC3 alerts; CISA and FTC guidance.
